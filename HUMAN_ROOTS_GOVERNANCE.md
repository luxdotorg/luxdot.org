# Human Roots Network — Governance & Genetic Rights Charter

## Status
Foundation specification. The project MUST NOT ingest raw genomic data for research until the governance gate below is satisfied.

## Mission
Support voluntary family-history documentation, responsible family reconnection, improved representation in genetics research, and public-interest science without turning genetic identity into a commodity.

## Genetic rights
1. Genetic data remains under the participant's control; participation never transfers ownership to LuxDot.
2. No sale or advertising use of genetic data.
3. No access for employment, insurance, immigration enforcement, policing, surveillance, or discriminatory profiling through this project.
4. Consent is granular: genealogy, relative matching, health research, and de-identified/pseudonymised population research are separate choices.
5. Family matching requires double opt-in before identities/contact details are exchanged.
6. Raw DNA files are never public.
7. Withdrawal must be supported. Participants must be told that withdrawal cannot necessarily retract data already irreversibly aggregated or lawfully incorporated into completed research.
8. A genetic association is not a diagnosis. Clinically significant findings require appropriate confirmation and qualified interpretation.
9. No claims of genetic purity or racial/national/religious superiority.
10. Data minimisation, purpose limitation, retention limits, encryption, auditability, and least-privilege access are mandatory.

## Special cases
Policies and trained human review are required for minors, adoption/donor conception, unexpected parentage, missing persons, conflict/displacement, vulnerable populations, deceased relatives, and relatives who do not want contact.

## Research governance gate
Before research genomic ingestion is enabled, require:
- independent ethics/scientific board;
- EU/GDPR legal and DPIA review;
- documented lawful basis and explicit-consent flows where required;
- controller/processor and international-transfer mapping;
- security architecture and incident-response review;
- retention/deletion/withdrawal procedures;
- researcher-access committee and auditable data-use agreements;
- policy for return/non-return of findings;
- independent review of claims made to participants;
- appropriate laboratory/clinical pathways for medically actionable findings.

## Data architecture principle
Phase 1 stores no raw genome. Store only minimum account/participant metadata, optional family-tree facts, consent records, kit sponsorship status, and non-identifying participation geography. Later research data must be segregated from identity/contact systems and protected with purpose-specific access.

## Kit solidarity programme
Companies, laboratories, universities and charities may sponsor tests. Sponsorship MUST NOT require participants to surrender ownership, accept unrelated research, waive privacy rights, or permit commercial resale. Allocation should use transparent public-interest criteria and avoid coercion.

## Scientific communication
Larger and more diverse datasets can improve statistical power and representation but do not automatically create vaccines, cures, diagnoses, or certain ancestry conclusions. Public language must preserve uncertainty.

## No Genetic Borders
Human population genetics reflects migration, mixture and shared ancestry. Project interfaces must not encode closed biological races or use ancestry estimates as proof of identity, citizenship, religion, ethnicity, superiority, or belonging.

# LuxDot Commons & Stewardship Policy --- Draft v0.1

**Status:** DRAFT FOR REVIEW --- not yet a legal license grant.

## Intent

LuxDot exists to preserve, connect, test, and share knowledge in service
of humanity. The project should prefer stewardship over ownership and
attribution over enclosure.

## Proposed policy architecture

### 1. Knowledge, research, writing, diagrams and original media

Preferred direction: **Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0
International (CC BY-SA 4.0)** for LuxDot-owned copyrightable
non-software material.

Why: - reuse and adaptation are allowed; - attribution is mandatory; -
adaptations remain under the same license, preserving the commons.

A stricter noncommercial license can be considered for selected
material, but that would make the material less open and creates
ambiguity around what counts as commercial use.

### 2. Software / source code

Do **not** use a Creative Commons license for software. Choose a
recognized software license after review.

Preferred candidate for LuxDot's reciprocity goal: **GNU AGPLv3** for
original LuxDot application code, subject to dependency/license
compatibility review.

### 3. Attribution and provenance

Reuse should preserve: - author/source attribution; - a link or
reference to the canonical LuxDot source when reasonably possible; -
license notice; - indication of modifications; - provenance/evidence
metadata where supplied.

### 4. Identity and anti-misrepresentation

Open licensing of content/code does not grant permission to impersonate
LuxDot or falsely claim that a modified work is an official LuxDot
publication.

The name, marks and official visual identity should be governed by a
separate **Trademark / Identity & Provenance Policy** whose purpose is
preventing confusion and falsified provenance, not monopolizing ideas.

### 5. Third-party material

LuxDot can license only rights it actually owns. Quotations,
photographs, manuscripts, datasets, religious texts, archival materials
and other third-party works retain their own rights/status and must be
marked accordingly.

### 6. Commercial activity and social return

The user's preferred ethical principle is: \> Knowledge may serve
humanity freely; commercial extraction should not become enclosure or
misrepresentation, and commercial benefit should return value to
society.

Important legal-design note: requiring a commercial user to donate a
percentage of revenue is **not compatible with standard Open Source
software licensing**, and adding custom ethical/noncommercial
restrictions creates a source-available/custom license rather than
standard Open Source.

Therefore the recommended model is: - keep the commons legally reusable
under established reciprocal licenses; - publish a separate **LuxDot
Social Return Pledge** asking commercial users to direct a meaningful
share of benefit to independently selected humanitarian/public-benefit
organizations; - do not route donations through LuxDot; - LuxDot
publishes no donation account and does not receive or custody charitable
funds; - where desired, commercial partners may publicly document
qualifying direct donations.

This pledge is ethical/social unless a later, lawyer-reviewed commercial
agreement makes it contractual for a specific partnership.

### 7. No donation custody

Proposed institutional principle: **LuxDot does not solicit or receive
donations for itself.** Where LuxDot connects donors with beneficiaries
or organizations, funds should move directly between them whenever
feasible. LuxDot acts as connector/documenter, not custodian.

### 8. Safety, illegality and abuse

A copyright license is a poor instrument for policing all harmful
conduct. Existing law, platform rules, product-specific safety controls,
contracts, and a public Responsible Use Policy should address misuse.
LuxDot should not promise that an open license can technically prevent
every abusive use.
